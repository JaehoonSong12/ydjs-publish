# 4. Geometry (기하)

## 4.1 Lines, Angles, and Shapes (선, 각, 도형)

### What You’ll Learn:

- Basics of points, lines, line segments, and rays  
- Types of angles (acute, right, obtuse, straight)  
- Basic 2D shapes and their properties

### Key Vocabulary:

- Line (선)  
- Angle (각)  
- Vertex (꼭짓점)  
- Polygon (다각형)

### Explanation:

- A **point (점)** shows a location.  
- A **line (선)** goes on forever in both directions.  
- A **line segment (선분)** has two endpoints.  
- A **ray (반직선)** starts at one point and goes forever in one direction.

### Types of Angles:

- **Acute angle (예각)**: less than 90°  
- **Right angle (직각)**: exactly 90°  
- **Obtuse angle (둔각)**: more than 90° but less than 180°  
- **Straight angle (평각)**: exactly 180°

### Did You Know?

> The word “geometry” comes from Greek and means “earth measurement.”

### Tips for Students:

- Use a protractor to measure angles accurately.  
- A triangle’s interior angles **always** add up to 180°.  
- Practice naming shapes by counting their sides.

---

## 4.2 Triangles and Quadrilaterals (삼각형과 사각형)

### What You’ll Learn:

- Classifying triangles by sides and angles  
- Identifying types of quadrilaterals

### Key Vocabulary:

- Triangle (삼각형)  
- Quadrilateral (사각형)  
- Equilateral triangle (정삼각형)  
- Isosceles triangle (이등변삼각형)  
- Scalene triangle (부등변삼각형)

### Types of Triangles:

By Sides:
- **Equilateral**: all sides are equal  
- **Isosceles**: two sides are equal  
- **Scalene**: no sides are equal

By Angles:
- **Acute triangle**: all angles are less than 90°  
- **Right triangle**: one angle is exactly 90°  
- **Obtuse triangle**: one angle is greater than 90°

### Types of Quadrilaterals:

- **Square (정사각형)**  
- **Rectangle (직사각형)**  
- **Rhombus (마름모)**  
- **Parallelogram (평행사변형)**  
- **Trapezoid (사다리꼴)**

### Did You Know?

> A square is a rectangle and a rhombus at the same time!

### Tips for Students:

- Draw and label shapes to learn their properties.  
- Use grid paper to check if sides are equal or parallel.

## 4.3 Circles and Parts of a Circle (원과 원의 부분)

### What You’ll Learn:

- Parts of a circle  
- How to find circumference and area

### Key Vocabulary:

- Circle (원)  
- Radius (반지름)  
- Diameter (지름)  
- Circumference (둘레)  
- Chord (현)  
- Center (중심)

### Circle Facts:

- **Radius**: from center to edge  
- **Diameter**: across the circle through the center (2 × radius)  
- **Circumference**: distance around the circle  
- **Area**: space inside the circle

### Formulas:

- Circumference = 2πr or πd  
- Area = πr²

### Did You Know?

> Pi (π) never ends—it starts as 3.14159 and keeps going!

### Tips for Students:

- Memorize key circle formulas.  
- Use π ≈ 3.14 in basic problems unless told otherwise.  
- Label radius and diameter clearly in drawings.

## 4.4 Area and Perimeter (넓이와 둘레)

### What You’ll Learn:

- How to find area and perimeter for basic shapes

### Key Vocabulary:

- Area (넓이)  
- Perimeter (둘레)  
- Base (밑변)  
- Height (높이)

### Formulas:

- Rectangle:  
  - Area = length × width  
  - Perimeter = 2(length + width)

- Triangle:  
  - Area = ½ × base × height

- Circle:  
  - Area = πr²  
  - Circumference = 2πr

### Did You Know?

> Perimeter measures the “fence,” and area measures the “floor.”

### Tips for Students:

- Always write the formula before solving.  
- Use the correct unit (cm² for area, cm for perimeter).  
- Check if the shape is regular or irregular.

## 4.5 Surface Area and Volume (겉넓이와 부피)

### What You’ll Learn:

- The difference between surface area and volume  
- How to calculate for 3D shapes

### Key Vocabulary:

- Surface Area (겉넓이)  
- Volume (부피)  
- Prism (기둥)  
- Cylinder (원기둥)  
- Cube (정육면체)  
- Rectangular Prism (직육면체)

### Formulas:

- Cube:  
  - Surface Area = 6 × side²  
  - Volume = side³

- Rectangular Prism:  
  - SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)  
  - Volume = l × w × h

- Cylinder:  
  - SA = 2πr² + 2πrh  
  - Volume = πr²h

### Did You Know?

> Volume tells you how much liquid fits inside a shape—like water in a bottle!

### Tips for Students:

- Use drawings to understand each part.  
- Always include units: cm² for surface area, cm³ for volume.  
- Break shapes into parts if they look complex.

## 4.6 Coordinate Plane (좌표평면)

### What You’ll Learn:

- What coordinates are and how to graph them  
- Understanding x- and y-axes

### Key Vocabulary:

- Coordinate Plane (좌표평면)  
- Origin (원점)  
- Axis (축)  
- Ordered Pair (좌표쌍)  
- Quadrant (사분면)

### Explanation:

- The **x-axis** runs left to right.  
- The **y-axis** runs up and down.  
- The **origin** is (0, 0) where they meet.  
- A point like (3, 2) means “3 across, 2 up.”

### Did You Know?

> The coordinate plane was invented by René Descartes, a French philosopher!

### Tips for Students:

- Go across (x) first, then up/down (y).  
- Plot points carefully and label them.  
- Know the four quadrants and their signs.

## 4.7 Transformations: Translation, Rotation, Reflection, Dilation (이동, 회전, 대칭, 확대/축소)

### What You’ll Learn:

- How shapes move or change size on the coordinate plane

### Key Vocabulary:

- Transformation (변환)  
- Translation (이동)  
- Rotation (회전)  
- Reflection (대칭)  
- Dilation (확대/축소)

### Explanation:

- **Translation**: Slide the shape to a new place.  
- **Rotation**: Turn the shape around a point.  
- **Reflection**: Flip the shape like a mirror.  
- **Dilation**: Make the shape bigger or smaller.

### Did You Know?

> Transformations are used in animations and video games!

### Tips for Students:

- Use graph paper to track movement.  
- Compare before and after shapes.  
- Tracing paper can help you visualize changes.
