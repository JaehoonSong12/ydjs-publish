## Part 1: Whole Numbers and Basic Operations

This part introduces the basics. We'll start with whole numbers and how they work. From there, we’ll move to operations and the rules that guide them.

---

### 2.1 Whole Numbers and Place Value

Whole numbers are values we use every day—counting numbers starting from zero. But each digit also carries power depending on where it sits. In our base‑10 system, places grow by tenfold as you move left.

For example, the number **3,450** means:
- 3 in the thousands place = 3,000  
- 4 in the hundreds place = 400  
- 5 in the tens place = 50  

You can express it as:
- **Standard Form**: `3450`  
- **Expanded Form**: `3000 + 400 + 50`  
- **Word Form**: *three thousand four hundred fifty*

Understanding place value helps you break apart numbers and build them back in meaningful ways.

---

### 2.2 Comparing and Rounding Numbers

**Comparing Numbers**  
We compare numbers using symbols: `>` (greater than), `<` (less than), and `=` (equal to). Read numbers left to right, digit by digit, to see which one is bigger or smaller.

**Rounding Numbers**  
To round, pick the place you need:
- Look at the next digit right.  
- If it’s **5 or more**, round up.  
- If it’s **4 or less**, round down.

Rounding makes numbers easier to work with when you need quick estimates.

---

### 2.3 Addition and Subtraction of Whole Numbers

**Addition** adds numbers together. We use the column method and carry over when sums exceed 9.

**Subtraction** finds the difference. If the top digit is smaller, borrow from the next left place.

Practice makes both methods smooth and reliable.

---

### 2.4 Multiplication and Division of Whole Numbers

**Multiplication** is repeated addition.  
Example: `3 × 4 = 12`

**Division** splits numbers into equal parts.  
Example: `12 ÷ 4 = 3`

Key terms:
- **Dividend**: the number being divided  
- **Divisor**: the number doing the dividing  
- **Quotient**: the result  
- **Remainder**: what’s left over

These concepts are vital for breaking down problems and checking work.

---

### 2.5 Order of Operations

Math has rules to avoid confusion. Follow **PEMDAS**:
- **P**arentheses  
- **E**xponents  
- **M**ultiplication and **D**ivision (left to right)  
- **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction (left to right)

Example:
5 + (3 × 2)²
→ 5 + 6²
→ 5 + 36 = 41

---

### 2.6 Estimation and Mental Math

Use **estimation** to get quick, reasonable answers:
- Round numbers for rough addition or multiplication.
- Good for checking calculations fast.

**Mental math** tips:
- Break numbers into parts (e.g. 49 = 50 – 1)
- Use doubles (e.g. 7 + 7 = 14)
- Rearrange operations (e.g. group additions first)

These methods boost speed and build number sense.

---

### 2.7 Properties of Operations

Know these useful rules:
- **Commutative**  
  - Addition: `a + b = b + a`  
  - Multiplication: `a × b = b × a`  
- **Associative**  
  - `(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)`  
  - `(a × b) × c = a × (b × c)`  
- **Distributive**  
  - `a × (b + c) = a × b + a × c`  
- **Identity**  
  - Addition: `a + 0 = a`  
  - Multiplication: `a × 1 = a`  
- **Zero Property**  
  - `a × 0 = 0`

These are shortcuts that simplify calculations and make algebra easier later on.

---

### 2.8 Problem Solving with Whole Numbers

Follow these steps:
1. **Understand** the problem.  
2. **Plan** your approach.  
3. **Solve** it carefully.  
4. **Check** your answer.

Watch for key words like *total, difference, product, quotient,* and *how many more*—they help you pick the right operation.

## Part 2: Variables and Algebra

Moving from arithmetic to algebra gives you tools to solve broader problems with symbols and structure. 

---

### 2.9 Variables and Algebraic Expressions

A **variable** (like `x`, `y`) stands for a number. An **expression** combines variables, numbers, and operations.

Example:
3x + 5 = 3 × x + 5
If `x = 2`, it becomes `3 × 2 + 5 = 11`

Fun fact: “Algebra” comes from the Arabic word *al‑jabr*, meaning *reunion of broken parts*.

---

### 2.10 Simplifying Expressions

Simplify by combining **like terms**:  
`2x + 3x = 5x`

Use the **distributive property**:
3(x + 4) = 3x + 12
Highlight like terms, manage signs, and work step by step.

---

### 2.11 Solving One-Step Equations

Use **inverse operations** to isolate variables. What you do on one side, do on the other.

Examples:
x + 4 = 9 → x = 5
5x = 20 → x = 4
Think of real life—5 items costing $20 means each item is $4.

---

### 2.12 Solving Multi-Step Equations

Simplify both sides first by combining like terms and using distribution. Then isolate the variable.

Example:
2(x + 3) = 14
2x + 6 = 14
2x = 8
x = 4

---

### 2.13 Inequalities

Use signs: `>`, `<`, `≥`, `≤`. Solve like equations, but **flip the sign** when multiplying or dividing by a negative.

Examples:
x – 3 > 2 → x > 5
–2x < 6 → x > –3
Graphing tip:
- Open circle = `<` or `>`
- Closed circle = `≤` or `≥`
