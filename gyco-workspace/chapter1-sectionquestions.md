# Chapter 1: Numbers & Operations — Practice Questions

## Section 1: Place Value

### Short Question
**Q1. What is the value of the digit 8 in the number 9836?**  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
**800** — it's in the **hundreds place**.  
</details>  

---

### Long Question  
**Q2. Write the number 5,041 in expanded form. Then explain what each digit means based on its place.**  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
**Expanded form:**  
5,000 + 0 + 40 + 1  
**Explanation:**  
- 5 = 5,000 (thousands)  
- 0 = 0 (hundreds)  
- 4 = 40 (tens)  
- 1 = 1 (ones)  
</details>  

---

### Challenge Question  
**Q3. Two numbers have the same digits but in a different order: 5,104 and 4,105. Which number is greater, and how do you know? Explain using place value.**  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
**5,104 is greater** because the **5** is in the **thousands place**, meaning 5,000.  
In 4,105, the **4** is in the thousands place, meaning 4,000.  
So: **5,104 > 4,105**  
</details>

## Section 2: Decimals and Fractions

### Short Question  
**Q1. Write the fraction \\(\\frac{3}{10}\\) as a decimal.**  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
**0.3** — Divide 3 by 10 to get the decimal.  
</details>

---

### Long Question  
**Q2. Convert the decimal 0.6 into a simplified fraction. Then explain each step.**  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
Step 1: Write 0.6 as \\(\\frac{6}{10}\\)  
Step 2: Simplify by dividing top and bottom by 2: \\(\\frac{6}{10} = \\frac{3}{5}\\)  
**Answer:** \\(\\frac{3}{5}\\)  
</details>

---

### Challenge Question  
**Q3. Emma says that the fraction \\(\\frac{4}{5}\\) is equal to the decimal 0.45. Is she correct? Explain your reasoning.**  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
**No**, because \\(\\frac{4}{5} = 0.8\\), not 0.45.  
\\(0.45 = \\frac{9}{20}\\), not the same as \\(\\frac{4}{5}\\).  
</details>

## Section 3: Comparing and Ordering Numbers

### Short Question  
**Q1. Which is greater: 5.03 or 5.3?**  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
**5.3 is greater** — it has more value in the tenths place.  
</details>

---

### Long Question  
**Q2. Put the following numbers in order from least to greatest: 0.199, \\(\\frac{1}{3}\\), 0.25, 0.2**  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
Convert to decimals:  
- 0.199  
- \\(\\frac{1}{3} \\approx 0.333\\)  
- 0.25  
- 0.2  

**Ordered:** 0.199 < 0.2 < 0.25 < \\(\\frac{1}{3}\\)  
</details>

---

## Section 5: Prime and Composite Numbers

### Short Question  
**Q1. Is 11 a prime or composite number?**  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
**Prime** — only two factors: 1 and 11.  
</details>

---

### Long Question  
**Q2. Identify all the prime numbers between 10 and 20. Explain how you know.**  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
**11, 13, 17, 19** — they each have exactly two factors: 1 and themselves.  
</details>

---

### Challenge Question  
**Q3. Why is the number 1 neither prime nor composite?**  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
It has only **one factor** (itself), not two or more. So it’s **neither**.  
</details>

## Section 6: GCF & LCM

### Short Question  
**Q1. What is the GCF of 8 and 12?**  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
**4** — the largest number that divides both.  
</details>

---

### Long Question  
**Q2. Find the LCM of 6 and 10 using prime factorization. Show your steps.**  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
- 6 = 2 × 3  
- 10 = 2 × 5  
Take the highest powers: 2 × 3 × 5 = **30**  
</details>

---

### Challenge Question  
**Q3. The GCF of two numbers is 7. One number is 35. What are possible values of the other number?**  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
The other number must be divisible by 7 but share no higher factor with 35.  
**Examples:** 7, 14, 21, 28, 49, etc.  
</details>
