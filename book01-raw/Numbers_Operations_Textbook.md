## Place Value (자리값)
### Concept Explanation
Place value is the value of each digit in a number based on its position. In the decimal system, each position is ten times the value of the position to its right.

| Position (from right) | Place    | Value               |
|-----------------------|----------|---------------------|
| 1                     | Ones     | 10^0 = 1            |
| 2                     | Tens     | 10^1 = 10           |
| 3                     | Hundreds | 10^2 = 100          |

For example, in the number 4,275:
- 5 is in the Ones place, value = 5  
- 7 is in the Tens place, value = 7 × 10 = 70  
- 2 is in the Hundreds place, value = 2 × 100 = 200  
- 4 is in the Thousands place, value = 4 × 1,000 = 4,000  

### Examples
1. In 9,836, the digit 8 is in the ____ place (Answer: Hundreds; value = 800).  
2. Decompose 5,104 into place values: 5,000 + 100 + 0 + 4.

### Practice Questions
1. Identify the place and value of the underlined digit:  
   a) 6,4_5_,321  
   b) 27_0_,489

2. Write these numbers in expanded form:  
   a) 3,209  
   b) 7,056

3. Fill in the blanks:  
   a) In the number 12,345, the digit 2 represents _____.  
   b) In 50,008, the digit 8 is in the _____ place.

### Answers
1a. Tens place, value = 50; 1b. Thousands place, value = 27,000  
2a. 3,000 + 200 + 0 + 9; 2b. 7,000 + 0 + 50 + 6  
3a. 2 × 1,000 = 2,000; 3b. Ones place

## 3. Chapter 2: Decimals and Fractions (소수와 분수)
### Concept Explanation
- A fraction represents a part of a whole and is written as \(\frac{numerator}{denominator}\).  
- A decimal is another way to represent fractions using base-ten notation, with digits to the right of the decimal point.

E.g., \(0.75 = \frac{75}{100} = \frac{3}{4}\).

### Examples
1. Convert \(\frac{4}{5}\) to a decimal: \(4 \div 5 = 0.8\).  
2. Write 0.125 as a fraction: \(0.125 = \frac{125}{1000} = \frac{1}{8}\).

### Practice Questions
1. Convert to decimal:  
   a) \(\frac{3}{10}\)  
   b) \(\frac{7}{20}\)

2. Convert to fraction (in simplest form):  
   a) 0.6  
   b) 0.04

3. Write as both fraction and decimal:  
   a) Three quarters  
   b) Nine tenths

### Answers
1a. 0.3; 1b. 0.35  
2a. \(\frac{6}{10} = \frac{3}{5}\); 2b. \(\frac{4}{100} = \frac{1}{25}\)  
3a. \(\frac{3}{4}, 0.75\); 3b. \(\frac{9}{10}, 0.9\)

## 4. Chapter 3: Comparing and Ordering Numbers (수 비교와 정렬)
### Concept Explanation
- Use symbols: > (greater than), < (less than), = (equal to).  
- To order numbers, arrange them from least to greatest or greatest to least.  
- Compare decimals by aligning decimal points; compare fractions by converting to like denominators or decimals.

### Examples
1. Compare: 0.7 __ 0.65 (Answer: >)  
2. Order smallest to largest: \(\tfrac{3}{4}\), 0.5, 0.75, \(\tfrac{2}{3}\) → 0.5, \(\tfrac{2}{3}\), \(\tfrac{3}{4}\), 0.75.

### Practice Questions
1. Fill in the correct symbol:  
   a) 5.03 __ 5.3  
   b) \(\tfrac{2}{5}\) __ \(\tfrac{3}{7}\)

2. Order from greatest to least:  
   0.2, \(\tfrac{1}{3}\), 0.25, 0.199

3. Which is larger: \(\tfrac{4}{9}\) or 0.44?

### Answers
1a. <; 1b. < (since 0.4 < ≈0.428)  
2. \(\tfrac{1}{3}\) (≈0.333), 0.25, 0.2, 0.199  
3. \(\tfrac{4}{9}\) ≈0.444… > 0.44

## 5. Chapter 4: Factors and Multiples (약수와 배수)
### Concept Explanation
- A factor is a number that divides another number exactly with no remainder.  
- A multiple is the product of a number and an integer.

E.g., factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12; multiples of 5: 5, 10, 15, 20, …

### Examples
1. List factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18.  
2. First five multiples of 7: 7, 14, 21, 28, 35.

### Practice Questions
1. Find all factors of 24.  
2. Write the first six multiples of 8.  
3. Is 9 a factor of 81? Explain.

### Answers
1. 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24  
2. 8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48  
3. Yes, because 81 ÷ 9 = 9 with no remainder.

## 6. Chapter 5: Prime Numbers & Composite Numbers (소수와 합성수)
### Concept Explanation
- A prime number has exactly two distinct factors: 1 and itself.  
- A composite number has more than two factors.  
- 1 is neither prime nor composite.

### Examples
1. Primes under 20: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19.  
2. Determine if 15 is prime or composite: composite (factors: 1, 3, 5, 15).

### Practice Questions
1. Identify which numbers are prime: 14, 17, 21, 23, 25.  
2. List all composite numbers between 10 and 20.  
3. Explain why 1 is neither prime nor composite.

### Answers
1. 17, 23 are prime; 14, 21, 25 are composite.  
2. 10, 12, 14, 15, 16, 18, 20  
3. Because 1 has only one factor (1 itself), not two.

## 7. Chapter 6: Greatest Common Factor (GCF) / Least Common Multiple (LCM) (최대공약수 / 최소공배수)
### Concept Explanation
- GCF of two numbers is the largest factor they share.  
- LCM is the smallest non-zero multiple common to both.

Methods:
- List prime factorizations and take common primes for GCF.  
- For LCM, take all primes at highest powers.

### Examples
1. Find GCF and LCM of 12 and 18.  
   - 12 = 2^2 × 3; 18 = 2 × 3^2  
   - GCF = 2^1 × 3^1 = 6  
   - LCM = 2^2 × 3^2 = 36

### Practice Questions
1. Find GCF(24, 36).  
2. Find LCM(8, 12).  
3. Compute GCF and LCM of 14 and 28.

### Answers
1. GCF = 12  
2. LCM = 24  
3. 14 = 2 × 7; 28 = 2^2 × 7; GCF = 2 × 7 = 14; LCM = 2^2 × 7 = 28
