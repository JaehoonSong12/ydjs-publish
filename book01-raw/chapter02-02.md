# 2. Expressions & Equations

## 2.1 Variables and Algebraic Expressions (문자와 식)

### What You’ll Learn

* Understand what variables are
* Learn how to write and interpret algebraic expressions

### Key Ideas

* A variable is a symbol (like x or y) that represents a number.
* An algebraic expression is a combination of numbers, variables, and operations (like +, −, ×, ÷).

**Example:**

* $3x + 5$ means “3 times x, plus 5.”
* If $x = 2$, then $3x + 5 = 3(2) + 5 = 11$

### Did You Know?

The word "algebra" comes from the Arabic word “al-jabr,” meaning “reunion of broken parts.” It was first used in a math book written over 1,200 years ago!

### Tips for Students

* Think of a variable as a blank box. What number could go in it?
* Try plugging in numbers to see how expressions change.
* Use color or symbols to match terms when writing expressions.

## 2.2 Simplifying Expressions (식의 간단화)

### What You’ll Learn

* Combine like terms
* Use the distributive property to simplify expressions

### Key Ideas

* Like terms have the same variable raised to the same power. You can combine them.
* The distributive property lets you multiply a number across a group: $a(b + c) = ab + ac$

**Example:**

* Simplify: $2x + 3x = 5x$
* Distribute: $3(x + 4) = 3x + 12$

### Did You Know?

Simplifying expressions helps make solving equations faster and easier. Even engineers and scientists use this every day in their calculations.

### Tips for Students

* Highlight or circle like terms before combining.
* Watch out for minus signs—keep them with the term.
* Rewrite expressions step-by-step to avoid mistakes.

## 2.3 Solving One-Step Equations (1단계 방정식)

### What You’ll Learn

* Solve equations that require only one operation to solve

### Key Ideas

* Use inverse operations to get the variable by itself.
* What you do to one side of the equation, do to the other.

**Example:**

* Solve: $x + 4 = 9$ → Subtract 4 from both sides → $x = 5$
* Solve: $5x = 20$ → Divide both sides by 5 → $x = 4$

### Did You Know?

Solving equations is like solving a puzzle. Each equation has a unique solution you can find by “undoing” operations in the reverse order.

### Tips for Students

* Always do the opposite operation to isolate the variable.
* Keep the equation balanced—do the same thing on both sides.
* Check your answer by plugging it back in.

## 2.4 Solving Multi-Step Equations (여러 단계 방정식)

### What You’ll Learn

* Solve equations that need more than one step
* Use combining like terms and distribution before solving

### Key Ideas

* First simplify both sides if needed (distribute and combine).
* Then isolate the variable using inverse operations.

**Example:**

* Solve: $2(x + 3) = 14$
* Step 1: Distribute → $2x + 6 = 14$
* Step 2: Subtract 6 → $2x = 8$
* Step 3: Divide by 2 → $x = 4$

### Did You Know?

Solving equations step-by-step builds your logic and problem-solving skills, which are useful in both math and everyday life.

### Tips for Students

* Write every step clearly—don’t skip!
* Simplify each side before solving.
* Always double-check by plugging your solution back in.

## 2.5 Inequalities (부등식)

### What You’ll Learn

* Understand and solve basic inequalities
* Learn how to graph solutions on a number line

### Key Ideas

* Inequality signs: > greater than, < less than, ≥ greater than or equal to, ≤ less than or equal to
* Solve just like equations, but remember:

  * If you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, flip the inequality sign.

**Example:**

* Solve: $x - 3 > 2$ → Add 3 to both sides → $x > 5$
* Solve: $-2x < 6$ → Divide by -2 and flip sign → $x > -3$

### Did You Know?

Inequalities are used in programming, business, and science to describe conditions or ranges, like “speed must be under 60 mph.”

### Tips for Students

* Treat inequalities like equations—except when multiplying/dividing by a negative.
* Graph your answers on a number line to check understanding.
* Don’t forget open (○) vs closed (●) circles on graphs!
