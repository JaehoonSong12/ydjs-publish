\section*{Sieve of Eratosthenes (1--100)}

\paragraph{Mathematical description.}
Fix \(n=100\). Let \(S=\{2,3,\dots,n\}\). For each integer \(p\) 
defined as the smallest element of \(S\) not yet processed, declare 
\(p\) \emph{prime} and remove from \(S\) every integer of the 
form \(kp\) with \(k\ge 2\). Continue this procedure while 
\(p^2 \le n\). The elements of \(S\) that remain after this process 
are precisely the prime numbers \(\le n\).

\paragraph{Algorithm (short).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Initialize the list \(2,3,\dots,n\).
  \item For each current smallest unprocessed \(p\) 
  (starting at \(p=2\)), remove all multiples 
  \(2p,3p,4p,\dots\) up to \(n\).
  \item Stop once \(p^2>n\) (i.e. \(p>\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor\)). 
  Remaining numbers are primes.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Note.} \(1\) is neither prime nor composite.
