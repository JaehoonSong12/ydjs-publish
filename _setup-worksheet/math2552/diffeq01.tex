  The given system of differential equations is $\vec{x}' = A\vec{x}$, where 
  \[
  A = \begin{bmatrix} 
  0 & 4 & -2 \\ 
  -1 & -4 & 1 \\ 
  0 & 0 & -2 
  \end{bmatrix}
  \]

  \subsection*{a. What are the eigenvalues?}

  The eigenvalues are determined by solving the characteristic equation $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$:
  \[
  \det \begin{bmatrix} 
  -\lambda & 4 & -2 \\ 
  -1 & -4-\lambda & 1 \\ 
  0 & 0 & -2-\lambda 
  \end{bmatrix} = 0
  \]
  The determinant is expanded along the third row:
  \[
  (-2 - \lambda) \det \begin{bmatrix} 
  -\lambda & 4 \\ 
  -1 & -4-\lambda 
  \end{bmatrix} = 0
  \]
  \[
  -(2 + \lambda) [-\lambda(-4 - \lambda) - (-4)] = 0
  \]
  \[
  -(2 + \lambda) [\lambda^2 + 4\lambda + 4] = 0
  \]
  \[
  -(2 + \lambda) (\lambda + 2)^2 = -(2 + \lambda)^3 = 0
  \]
  The eigenvalue is found to be $\lambda = -2$ with an algebraic multiplicity of $k = 3$.

  \subsection*{b. What is/are the defect(s) of the eigenvalue(s)?}

  The defect of an eigenvalue is defined as the difference between its algebraic multiplicity and its geometric multiplicity. The geometric multiplicity is the dimension of the eigenspace, calculated as the nullity of $(A - \lambda I)$. For $\lambda = -2$:
  \[
  A - (-2)I = A + 2I = \begin{bmatrix} 
  2 & 4 & -2 \\ 
  -1 & -2 & 1 \\ 
  0 & 0 & 0 
  \end{bmatrix}
  \]
  The rows of $A + 2I$ are linearly dependent, as $R_1 = -2R_2$. The rank of the matrix is 1. Thus, the geometric multiplicity is:
  \[
  \text{nullity}(A + 2I) = 3 - \text{rank}(A + 2I) = 3 - 1 = 2
  \]
  The defect $d$ is calculated as:
  \[
  d = \text{algebraic multiplicity} - \text{geometric multiplicity} = 3 - 2 = 1
  \]
  The eigenvalue $\lambda = -2$ has a defect of 1.

  \subsection*{c. Find the general solution of $\vec{x}' = A\vec{x}$.}
\begin{define}{Jordan Chain}
  A Jordan chain of length $k$ associated with an eigenvalue $\lambda$ of a matrix $A$ is defined as a sequence of non-zero vectors $\{\vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2, \dots, \vec{v}_k\}$ that satisfy the following relations:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $(A - \lambda I)\vec{v}_1 = \vec{0}$ (where $\vec{v}_1$ is a rank-1 eigenvector).
    \item $(A - \lambda I)\vec{v}_2 = \vec{v}_1$ (where $\vec{v}_2$ is a rank-2 generalized eigenvector).
    \item $(A - \lambda I)\vec{v}_j = \vec{v}_{j-1}$ for $2 \leq j \leq k$.
  \end{itemize}
  This chain is utilized to construct linearly independent solutions for systems of differential equations when the geometric multiplicity of an eigenvalue is less than its algebraic multiplicity.
\end{define}



\begin{define}{Jordan Chain Solution}
  To derive the solution form $\vec{x}(t) = e^{\lambda t}(t\vec{v}_1 + \vec{w})$ for a chain of length 2, the proposed solution is substituted into the differential equation $\vec{x}' = A\vec{x}$.
  
  The derivative of the proposed solution is calculated using the product rule:
  \[
  \vec{x}'(t) = \lambda e^{\lambda t}(t\vec{v}_1 + \vec{w}) + e^{\lambda t}(\vec{v}_1) = e^{\lambda t}(\lambda t \vec{v}_1 + \lambda \vec{w} + \vec{v}_1)
  \]
  
  The right side of the differential equation is expanded as:
  \[
  A\vec{x}(t) = A(e^{\lambda t}(t\vec{v}_1 + \vec{w})) = e^{\lambda t}(t A\vec{v}_1 + A\vec{w})
  \]
  
  From the definition of a Jordan chain of length 2 ($\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{w}$), it is known that $A\vec{v}_1 = \lambda \vec{v}_1$ and $(A - \lambda I)\vec{w} = \vec{v}_1$, which implies $A\vec{w} = \lambda \vec{w} + \vec{v}_1$. Substituting these relations into the right side:
  \[
  A\vec{x}(t) = e^{\lambda t}(t \lambda \vec{v}_1 + \lambda \vec{w} + \vec{v}_1)
  \]
  
  Since $\vec{x}'(t) = A\vec{x}(t)$ is satisfied by this expression, the form $\vec{x}(t) = e^{\lambda t}(t\vec{v}_1 + \vec{w})$ is confirmed as a valid solution.
\end{define}
  Since the defect is 1, a chain of generalized eigenvectors must be constructed. Because $(A+2I)^2$ results in the zero matrix, any vector $\vec{w}$ that is not in the kernel of $(A+2I)$ can serve as a generalized eigenvector of rank 2. Let $\vec{w}$ be chosen as:
  \[
  \vec{w} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}
  \]
  The associated eigenvector $\vec{v}_1$ is generated by:
  \[
  \vec{v}_1 = (A+2I)\vec{w} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 & -2 \\ -1 & -2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}
  \]
  A second linearly independent eigenvector $\vec{v}_2$ is chosen from the kernel of $(A+2I)$, such that $2v_x + 4v_y - 2v_z = 0$. Let:
  \[
  \vec{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}
  \]
  The general solution is then expressed as:
  \[
  \vec{x}(t) = c_1 e^{-2t} \vec{v}_1 + c_2 e^{-2t} (t \vec{v}_1 + \vec{w}) + c_3 e^{-2t} \vec{v}_2
  \]
  Substituting the specific vectors:
  \[
  \vec{x}(t) = c_1 e^{-2t} \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + c_2 e^{-2t} \left( t \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \right) + c_3 e^{-2t} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}
  \]
