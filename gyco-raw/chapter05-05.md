# Chapter 5: Data & Statistics (자료와 통계)

In this chapter, you’ll explore how we collect, organize, and understand numbers that represent real-life information. This is where math meets the world—like figuring out average test scores, reading charts, or predicting outcomes!

---

## 5.1 Mean, Median, Mode, Range (평균, 중앙값, 최빈값, 범위)

These are all ways to describe a **set of data**.

### 📊 Mean (평균)
Add all the values together and divide by how many there are. This is what most people think of as the “average.”

**Example:**  
(2 + 4 + 6) ÷ 3 = 12 ÷ 3 = **4**

### 📏 Median (중앙값)
The **middle number** when all numbers are listed in order.

**Example:**  
\[2, 5, 7] → Median = **5**  
\[1, 3, 5, 7] → Median = (3 + 5)/2 = **4**

### 🔁 Mode (최빈값)
The number that **appears most often** in a set.

**Example:**  
\[1, 2, 2, 3] → Mode = **2**  
\[1, 1, 2, 3, 3] → Modes = **1 and 3**

### 📐 Range (범위)
The difference between the **largest** and **smallest** values.

**Example:**  
\[1, 5, 8] → Range = 8 - 1 = **7**

---

## 5.2 Line Plots, Bar Graphs, Histograms (그래프 종류)

### 📍 Line Plot (선 그림)
Uses **dots or x’s** to show how many times each number appears. It’s great for small sets of data.


### 📊 Bar Graph (막대 그래프)
Uses **bars** to compare different categories.

**Example:**  
- Apples: 5  
- Bananas: 3  
- Grapes: 7

Each category gets a bar showing how many.

### 🧱 Histogram (히스토그램)
Looks like a bar graph, but the bars represent **intervals (ranges)** instead of categories.

**Example:**  
Ages of students:  
- 0–5 years → 3 students  
- 6–10 years → 7 students  
- 11–15 years → 6 students

Great for large amounts of data grouped into ranges!

---

## 5.3 Probability Basics (확률의 기초)

**Probability (확률)** means how likely something is to happen.

### 🎲 Basic Formula:
**Probability = Favorable outcomes ÷ Total possible outcomes**

**Example:**  
Rolling a 3 on a 6-sided die:  
Only one 3 → **1/6**

### 🔒 Special Cases:
- **Certain (확실)** = 1 or 100%  
- **Impossible (불가능)** = 0 or 0%  
- **Equally likely** = 50% → like flipping a fair coin

---

## 5.4 Experimental vs. Theoretical Probability (실험적 확률 vs 이론적 확률)

### 🧠 Theoretical Probability (이론적 확률)
What should happen based on math and logic.

**Example:**  
Probability of getting heads when flipping a coin = **1/2**

### 🧪 Experimental Probability (실험적 확률)
What really happens when you do an experiment.

**Example:**  
Flip a coin 10 times, get 7 heads →  
Experimental probability = **7/10**

The more trials you do, the closer experimental probability gets to theoretical probability.

---

## 📌 Tips for Students

- Always **organize your data** clearly before starting.
- Line plots = for small, exact data.  
- Histograms = for grouped, large data.
- Practice flipping coins or rolling dice to understand **real-world probability**.
- Know the **difference** between average types—don’t assume mean = median = mode!

---

## 💡 Did You Know?

- The word **"average"** usually means the **mean**, but statisticians use **three types**: mean, median, and mode!
- **Weather forecasts**, **sports predictions**, and even **video games** all use probability math.
- In Korean, “통계” (statistics) is used often in news and government reports to help people understand trends and decisions.
