# Unit 6: Integers & Rational Numbers (정수와 유리수)

## 6.1 Positive and Negative Numbers (양수와 음수)

### What You’ll Learn

* Understand what positive and negative numbers mean  
* Compare and place integers on a number line  

### Key Ideas

* Positive numbers are greater than zero (e.g., 1, 2, 3…)  
* Negative numbers are less than zero (e.g., −1, −2, −3…)  
* Zero is neither positive nor negative.  

**Example:**

* On a number line:  
  $-3 < -1 < 0 < 2 < 5$  
* -10 is less than -2 because it is farther left on the number line.  

### Did You Know?

Negative numbers were once thought to be “imaginary” by ancient mathematicians. Now they’re used in temperatures, banking, and more!

### Tips for Students

* Think of temperature: 10°C is warmer than -5°C.  
* Use number lines to help compare.  
* Remember: the farther left, the smaller the number.

---

## 6.2 Absolute Value (절댓값)

### What You’ll Learn

* Understand and find the absolute value of a number  

### Key Ideas

* Absolute value is the distance from zero, always positive.  
* Symbol: $|a|$ means “the absolute value of a.”  

**Example:**

* $|5| = 5$  
* $|-5| = 5$  
* $|0| = 0$  

### Did You Know?

The idea of absolute value comes from geometry—it’s like measuring how far a number is from zero, no matter the direction.

### Tips for Students

* Ignore the sign—just count the distance.  
* Think of it like walking: 5 steps left or right is still 5 steps from home.

---

## 6.3 Adding/Subtracting Integers (정수의 더하기와 빼기)

### What You’ll Learn

* Add and subtract positive and negative numbers  

### Key Ideas

* Same signs: add and keep the sign  
* Different signs: subtract and keep the sign of the larger number  

**Example:**

* $3 + (-5) = -2$  
* $-6 + (-4) = -10$  
* $7 - 2 = 5$  
* $-3 - 5 = -8$  

### Did You Know?

Adding and subtracting integers is like managing money—you gain or lose value based on what’s added or taken away.

### Tips for Students

* Use counters or a number line when starting out.  
* Remember “adding a negative” is like subtracting.  
* Rewrite subtraction as addition:  
  $4 - 7$ becomes $4 + (-7) = -3$

---

## 6.4 Multiplying/Dividing Integers (정수의 곱하기와 나누기)

### What You’ll Learn

* Multiply and divide positive and negative numbers  

### Key Ideas

* Same signs → Positive answer  
* Different signs → Negative answer  

**Example:**

* $(-3) × (-4) = 12$  
* $(-3) × 4 = -12$  
* $12 ÷ (-3) = -4$  
* $(-12) ÷ (-3) = 4$  

### Did You Know?

Two negatives make a positive in multiplication and division—but not in addition! That’s why rules are important to memorize.

### Tips for Students

* Say: “Same signs, positive. Different signs, negative.”  
* Ignore signs at first—multiply or divide normally, then apply the rule.  
* Double-check your answer with a calculator if needed.

## 6.5 Rational Numbers and Number Line (유리수와 수직선)

### What You’ll Learn

* Understand rational numbers  
* Plot rational numbers on a number line  

### Key Ideas

* Rational numbers include integers, fractions, and decimals that can be written as a ratio (like ½, -4, or 0.75).  
* They can be placed on a number line in order.  

**Example:**

* Rational numbers: $-2, \frac{1}{2}, 0.75, -\frac{3}{4}$  
* $-1 < -\frac{3}{4} < 0 < \frac{1}{2} < 0.75$  

### Did You Know?

The word “rational” comes from “ratio”—these are numbers that can be written as one number over another.

### Tips for Students

* Convert decimals to fractions or vice versa to compare.  
* Use benchmarks like 0, ½, and 1 when estimating on number lines.  
* Practice plotting points to get comfortable.
