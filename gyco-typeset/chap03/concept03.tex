\section{Unit Rate}

A \textbf{unit rate} is a rate where the second quantity is exactly \textbf{1}. It tells you how much of something there is \textbf{per one unit} of something else.

**Example:**  
If 3 notebooks cost \$6, the unit rate is:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \$6 ÷ 3 = \textbf{\$2 per notebook}
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{How to Find a Unit Rate}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Divide the first number by the second.
  \item Make sure the second number becomes 1.
\end{enumerate}

**Examples:**
\begin{itemize}
  \item 120 miles in 4 hours → \textbf{120 ÷ 4 = 30 miles per hour}
  \item 8 apples for \$4 → \textbf{\$4 ÷ 8 = \$0.50 per apple}
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Real-Life Uses}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Grocery stores often list unit prices on shelf tags to help shoppers compare.
  \item Runners track their pace (minutes per mile) to measure speed.
  \item Restaurants calculate price per serving to set menu prices.
\end{itemize}

Unit rates are super helpful when budgeting, comparing phone plans, or deciding which product gives you the best deal!

\subsection{Practice Questions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Short Question} \\
  If 5 oranges cost \$3, what is the unit rate per orange?
  
  \textbf{Answer:} \textbf{\$0.60 per orange} — Divide \$3 by 5: $\$3 \div 5 = \$0.60$.

  \item \textbf{Long Question} \\
  A car travels 200 miles in 4 hours. Find the unit rate in miles per hour.
  
  \textbf{Answer:} \\
  Unit rate = $\frac{200 \text{ miles}}{4 \text{ hours}} = 50$ miles per hour \\
  \textbf{Answer:} 50 miles per hour

  \item \textbf{Challenge Question} \\
  Store A sells 12 apples for \$6, and Store B sells 8 apples for \$4. Which store has the better unit price?
  
  \textbf{Answer:} \\
  Store A: $\frac{\$6}{12 \text{ apples}} = \$0.50$ per apple \\
  Store B: $\frac{\$4}{8 \text{ apples}} = \$0.50$ per apple \\
  \textbf{Answer:} Both stores have the same unit price of \$0.50 per apple
\end{enumerate}
