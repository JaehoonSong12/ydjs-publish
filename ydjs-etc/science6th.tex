\section*{Glossary}

\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.28\textwidth} p{0.68\textwidth}@{}}
\textbf{Mineral} & a naturally occurring, inorganic solid that has a crystal structure and a definite chemical composition. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Rock} & a naturally formed solid made of one or more minerals that make up the earth's crust. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Metallic} & consisting of metal. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Moh's Hardness Scale} & a scale ranking ten minerals from softest to hardest; used in testing the hardness of minerals. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Luster} & the way a mineral reflects light. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Hardness} & a measure of how easily a mineral can be scratched. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Streak} & the color of a mineral's powder. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Density} & the amount of mass in a given volume. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Sediment} & small, solid pieces of material that come from rocks. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Weathering} & the chemical and physical processes that break down rock at Earth's surface. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Erosion} & condition in which the earth's surface is worn away by the action of water and wind. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Deposition} & process in which sediment is laid down in new locations. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Fossils} & the preserved trace, imprint, or remains of a plant or animal that lived long ago. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Igneous Rock} & a type of rock that forms from the cooling of molten rock at or below the surface. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Sedimentary Rock} & rock that forms from other rocks or plant/animal remains by compaction and cementation. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Metamorphic Rock} & rock that has been changed by heat and pressure. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Rock Cycle} & sequence of events in which rocks are formed, destroyed, altered, and reformed by geological processes. \\[6pt]

\textbf{Color} & an easily observed physical property used in identifying minerals. \\
\end{tabular}


\section*{Key Vocabulary}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.28\textwidth} p{0.68\textwidth}@{}}
\textbf{Mineral} & A naturally occurring, inorganic solid with a crystalline structure and a definite chemical composition. \\[6pt]
\textbf{Rock} & A naturally formed solid made of one or more minerals. \\[6pt]
\textbf{Metallic} & Having the appearance or properties of metal. \\[6pt]
\textbf{Mohs Hardness Scale} & A relative scale (1--10) used to rank minerals by scratch resistance (talc = 1, diamond = 10). \\[6pt]
\textbf{Luster} & The way a mineral reflects light (e.g., metallic, glassy, pearly). \\[6pt]
\textbf{Hardness} & A measure of how easily a mineral can be scratched (tested with Mohs scale). \\[6pt]
\textbf{Streak} & The color of a mineral's powdered form (produced by rubbing on a streak plate). \\[6pt]
\textbf{Density} & Mass per unit volume; helpful for identification (heft test). \\[6pt]
\textbf{Cleavage} & Tendency of a mineral to break along flat, planar surfaces. \\[6pt]
\textbf{Fracture} & Way a mineral breaks when it does not cleave (uneven, conchoidal, etc.). \\[6pt]
\textbf{Sediment} & Small solid pieces of material produced by weathering and erosion. \\[6pt]
\textbf{Weathering} & Chemical and physical breakdown of rock at Earth's surface. \\[6pt]
\textbf{Erosion} & Movement of weathered material by water, wind, or ice. \\[6pt]
\textbf{Deposition} & Laying down of sediment in a new location. \\[6pt]
\textbf{Fossils} & Preserved traces or remains of ancient organisms. \\[6pt]
\textbf{Igneous Rock} & Rock formed by cooling of molten material (magma or lava). \\[6pt]
\textbf{Sedimentary Rock} & Rock formed by compaction/cementation of sediments or of biological remains. \\[6pt]
\textbf{Metamorphic Rock} & Rock changed by heat and pressure from a previous rock type. \\[6pt]
\textbf{Rock Cycle} & The set of processes that form, change, destroy, and reform rocks. \\[6pt]
\end{tabular}

\section*{Key Points and Diagnostic Tests}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Mineral vs rock:} Minerals are single, naturally occurring, inorganic, crystalline substances with a fixed or narrowly variable chemical formula. Rocks are aggregates of one or more minerals (or mineraloids). Examples: quartz (mineral) vs sandstone (rock).
  \item \textbf{Essential identification tests:}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \emph{Color:} quick clue but often unreliable alone.
      \item \emph{Streak:} color of powdered mineral; more reliable than surface color.
      \item \emph{Luster:} metallic, glassy, pearly, etc.; describes light reflection.
      \item \emph{Hardness:} use Mohs scale (1--10). Scratch test distinguishes soft vs hard minerals.
      \item \emph{Cleavage vs fracture:} look for flat planar breaks (cleavage) versus irregular breaks (fracture).
      \item \emph{Density/heft:} relative heaviness can separate similar-looking minerals (e.g., gold vs pyrite).
      \item \emph{Other tests:} acid reaction (calcite), magnetism (magnetite), taste/smell only in controlled classroom settings.
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Common pitfalls (concept checks from the text):}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Materials from living organisms (ivory, amber) or man-made materials (Bakelite, Portland cement) are \emph{not} minerals.
      \item Volcanic glass (obsidian) lacks a crystalline structure and so is usually not classified as a mineral.
      \item Sandstone is a rock (mixture of minerals like quartz and feldspar), not a single mineral because it is not a pure substance with a fixed formula.
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Example identifications from the sheet:}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Fluorite: inorganic solid with formula CaF$_2$; shows glassy luster and white streak $\rightarrow$ qualifies as a mineral.
      \item Chrysoberyl: BeAl$_2$O$_4$, crystalline, hardness about 8.5 $\rightarrow$ mineral.
      \item Gold vs pyrite: use hardness and streak. Gold is soft (approx. 2.5--3) and leaves a golden streak; pyrite is harder (6--6.5) and typically leaves a dark/greenish-black streak.
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Teaching/assessment notes:} multiple-choice items should emphasize the mineral definition (naturally occurring, inorganic, solid, crystalline, definite composition) and common identification tests. Use short lab tests (streak plate, glass scratch, visual cleavage) to reinforce concepts.
\end{enumerate}
